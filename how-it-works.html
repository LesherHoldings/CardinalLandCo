How it works

A free assessment, step by step.

Our land assessment is free for Pennsylvania landowners. It begins with a 15-minute call. It ends, if the land fits, with introductions only to vetted companies — and those companies pay our fee. You do not.

Step one

Tell us about your land

It starts with a phone call. Most take about 15 minutes. You can call us, or send the form and we will call you. There is nothing to prepare and nothing to sign.

We will ask a short list of plain questions:

  • Where the land is: the county and township, or the nearest town.
  • How many acres, roughly.
  • What is on it now: crops, pasture, woods, buildings, a house.
  • Whether there is road frontage, and whether power lines run nearby or across it.
  • Whether a land lease offer or a letter has already arrived, and from whom.
  • Whether your family owns the mineral and gas rights under the land, if you know.

If you do not know an answer, say so. Many families are not sure who holds the rights under their ground. We look at what the county record shows, and we tell you when a title search by an attorney is the only way to settle it.

Good to have handy

None of this is required. It just makes the call go faster.

  • Your deed, or the tax parcel number from your county tax bill.
  • Any letter or offer you have received, with the company name and the date.
  • A rough idea of the acreage in fields, pasture, and woods.
  • Anything you already know about the mineral or gas rights.

Step two

We assess every opportunity

After the call, we go to work. We look at your land the way each kind of company would, and we look at all four at once: solar, natural gas, minerals, and timber.

For solar, that means acreage, the lay of the ground, and how far the fields sit from a substation or transmission line. For gas and minerals, it means which rights go with the land and who, if anyone, is leasing in your county. For timber, it means what is standing in the woods and whether it is worth having a consulting forester walk it.

We pull the public records: the deed, the parcel map, and the leases or rights-of-way on record at the county courthouse. We check which companies are active in your part of the state and which are only sending letters.

Then we call you back and tell you plainly what is worth pursuing and what is not. If nothing fits, we say so, and that is the end of it. You owe us nothing.

What we look at

Acreage
How much open ground, how much woods, and how it is laid out.
Location
County, township, and which companies are active there.
Utility access
Road frontage, nearby power lines, substations, and pipelines.
Timber
What is standing in the woods, and whether it is worth a consulting forester’s look.
Mineral status
What the county record shows about whether the gas and mineral rights go with the land, and whether a lease is already on record.

Step three

We introduce you only to vetted companies

If there is real interest in your land, we introduce you to a company we have checked out. Only then, and only with your say-so.

“Vetted” is a word that gets used loosely. Here is what it means in our office. We find out who the company is and who stands behind it. We look at what it has actually built, not what it has announced. We check whether it is still active in Pennsylvania or has moved on. A company that cannot answer those questions does not get introduced to you.

You decide whether to take the meeting. If you do, the company deals with you directly from there. If a lease or sale closes, the company pays our fee. You never receive a bill from us.

One thing we want to be plain about: we do not negotiate the lease for you, and we do not review or approve it. Before you sign anything, have your own attorney read it. Any company worth dealing with expects that.

Questions worth asking any company

  • Who owns the company, and who is paying for the project?
  • What have you built in Pennsylvania, and where can I see it?
  • How long does the lease run, and what happens when it ends?
  • Who pays the property taxes, including any Clean and Green roll-back?
  • How will the land be restored, and who pays for it?
  • May my attorney review the lease before I sign?

Write the answers down. They should match what the lease says.

Where our work stops

What we do not do

It is worth knowing where our work stops, so there are no surprises later.

  • We do not charge landowners. Not for the call, not for the assessment, and not for the introduction.
  • We are not your agent, and we do not negotiate the lease for you. The terms are between you, the company, and your attorney.
  • We do not give legal, financial, or appraisal advice. For that you want your own attorney, your accountant, and a licensed appraiser.
  • We do not share your information without your say-so. Your name and how to reach you stay in our office until you ask us to make an introduction.
  • We are not a solar company, a gas company, or a timber buyer. We do not build, drill, or cut. We assess land and make introductions.

Questions landowners ask

Plain answers to common questions

  • What does this cost me?

    Nothing. The call, the assessment, and any introduction are free to the landowner, and there is no bill later. If nothing on your land fits, we tell you so, and that is the end of it.

  • Who pays you?

    The company does. If a landowner we introduce signs a lease or completes a sale, the company pays our fee. If nothing closes, no one pays us. We tell you this up front because you should know how anyone at the table is paid, including us.

  • Do I have to sign anything?

    No. There is no contract with us, no fee agreement, and no exclusive arrangement. You can stop at any point, for any reason. Anything you do sign would be with a company, not with us — an option, a lease, a right-of-way, or a sale agreement — and each one is a decision to make with your own attorney.

  • What if I already got an offer?

    Bring it to the call. An offer tells us a great deal: which company is interested, what it is after, and how it sees your land. We look at the same ground with fresh eyes and tell you whether that offer is the only option or just the first one to reach your mailbox.

    We do not negotiate the offer for you, and we do not tell you what to accept. We give you a second opinion on what your land could do, so you are not deciding with one letter in your hand. If the letter carries a deadline, tell us. A 15-minute call fits inside almost any deadline.

  • How long does a solar lease last?

    Longer than most people expect. Solar land leases in Pennsylvania commonly run 25 years or more, often with options for the company to extend. Many begin with a development period of several years, while the company studies the site and seeks permits and a grid connection, before anything is built. Ask the company to walk you through each phase, and have your attorney confirm what the lease says about each one.

  • What happens to my farm?

    You stay the owner of the land throughout. The one exception is an outright sale of mineral rights, where you keep the surface and the buyer owns what is under it. Beyond that, it depends on the kind of lease. A solar lease usually takes the leased fields out of production for the life of the lease, while the rest of the farm keeps operating. A gas lease may bring a well pad, an access road, or a pipeline onto part of the property, or no surface work at all if the gas is reached from a well on nearby land. The lease sets out what the company may do on the surface. A timber sale is a one-time harvest; the woods are yours before and after.

    Whatever you are considering, ask the company how the land will be restored and who pays for decommissioning when a project ends, and have your attorney confirm that the lease says the same thing.

  • Do you only work in Lancaster County?

    No — we assess land anywhere in Pennsylvania, and travel to the property when it’s needed. Our office is in Lancaster County, and our work covers every county in the state.

  • What if my land is not a fit?

    Then we tell you so, plainly, and we tell you why. It may be the distance to power lines, the lay of the ground, the rights attached to the deed, or simply that no company is active in your county right now. Conditions change. Transmission lines get built and companies move into new counties, so you are welcome to call again in a year or two. Either way, you owe us nothing.

  • What about Clean and Green?

    Clean and Green is Pennsylvania’s program that taxes enrolled farm and forest land on its use value rather than its market value. When enrolled land changes use, as it can when fields go under solar panels, the county can charge roll-back taxes for earlier years. Ask any company who pays those taxes, and get the answer in writing. Your county assessment office and your attorney can tell you how the program applies to your parcel.
